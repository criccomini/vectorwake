# What a 1998 zone's map is made of

Measured from one `.lvl` map of the original's, converted to our tile classes
and then counted. The point of counting was to be able to build our own map
that plays in the same register without any of its geometry, so what follows
is deliberately a list of quantities rather than a description of any
particular structure on it.

The distinction matters and is the reason this file exists. A map is a drawing
and belongs to whoever drew it. That three per cent of it is wall is a fact
about the drawing, and a fact is not the drawing. Everything below is the
former. `sim/tools/mapgen.c` reads these numbers and produces a map of its
own; [design/maps.md](../design/maps.md) describes what it does with them.

## The headline: it is not a maze

| | |
|---|---|
| Grid | 1024 x 1024 tiles, 16 px each |
| Wall | 3.05% of the interior |
| Everything else | open |

Ninety-seven per cent of the field is empty. Whatever else the original was
doing, it was not building corridors. A pilot's default state is flying in the
open with something in the distance, and every structure on the map is a thing
standing in a field rather than a wall of a room the field is inside.

That single number decides more about how a map plays than any other. At three
per cent you cannot corner anybody, cover is always a short burn away, and a
fight is broken off by flying rather than by finding a door.

## Wall is thin

| Run length | Share of wall runs |
|---|---|
| 1 tile | 77.3% |
| 2 tiles | 10.3% |
| 3 tiles | 1.8% |
| 4 or more | 10.6% |

Four fifths of it is a single tile thick. Structures are outlines, not
masses: a hollow box rather than a filled one, a line rather than a slab. The
tail past four tiles is mostly the long straight walls of the largest
structures.

Thin wall is a gameplay decision disguised as an art decision. A one-tile wall
is cover you can be killed through the moment you stop moving, since a bomb
landing on the far side of it still reaches you, and it costs a bouncing
bullet nothing to come back around.

## Open space, and why the distribution has two humps

| Percentile of open runs | Tiles |
|---|---|
| 25th | 13 |
| 50th | 23 |
| 75th | 68 |
| 90th | 179 |
| longest | 1008 |

Read that as two populations rather than one. Short runs are gaps inside a
structure or between two structures standing together. Long runs are the
lanes between one group of structures and the next. A map generated by
scattering things evenly produces the middle of this table and neither end,
which was the first thing our own generator got wrong and the most obvious
one to look at.

For scale: a client holds 640 world pixels on the short axis, so a screen is
40 tiles and the radar reaches 60. A 179-tile run is three screens of nothing.

## Even across the map, uneven inside a screen

The single most useful thing measured here, and the one a generator gets
wrong by default. Sort the tiles into square blocks and ask how much wall is
in each:

| Block | Spread of wall % (sd/mean) | Blocks with no wall at all |
|---|---|---|
| 32 tiles | 1.00 | 21.7% |
| 64 tiles | 0.65 | 3.6% |
| 128 tiles | 0.42 | 0.0% |

Read the bottom row twice. Across 128-tile squares there is **not one empty
square on the map**: the emptiest is 1.6% wall and the fullest 7.3%. Across
32-tile squares, a fifth are empty and one holds 25%.

So the map is close to uniform at the scale of a quarter of the map, and wildly
uneven at the scale of a screen. Every part of it has something in it; what
varies is how the something is arranged once you are close enough to fly
through it.

Put the other way round, from the open ground:

| Open ground more than N tiles from any wall | Share of open ground |
|---|---|
| 10 tiles | 40.9% |
| 20 tiles | 15.3% |
| 30 tiles | 5.2% |
| 40 tiles | 1.7% |

Two fifths of the field is genuinely open at close range, and almost none of
it is open at long range. There is no acre of nothing anywhere on this map.

## Structures

Blocking tiles clustered together when within six tiles of each other, which
is roughly how the eye groups them:

| | |
|---|---|
| Count | 774 |
| Span, 25th percentile | 4 tiles |
| Span, median | 13 tiles |
| Span, 75th percentile | 24 tiles |
| Span, 90th percentile | 41 tiles |
| Span, largest | 310 tiles |
| Nearest neighbour, median | 15 tiles |

Most of them are small. A handful are enormous, and those few are what a
player navigates by: a map whose structures are all one size is a map where
every direction looks the same.

They also arrive in groups. Two to six of a kind at one size, in a row or a
small grid, then nothing for a long way. That is what produces a
fifteen-tile median spacing at the same time as hundred-tile lanes.

## The furniture

| Class | Count |
|---|---|
| Safe | 526 tiles in 9 separate zones |
| Door | 820 tiles in 28 runs across 6 channels |
| Wormhole | 1 |
| Spawn | 16, eight per side |

Nine safe zones rather than two. A player who wants out of a fight is never
far from an exit, and no single one of them can be camped to deny the room.
Each is a filled rectangle, nine wide and eight tall at the median, though
their mean of 58 tiles against that median of 72 says a few are half size.
They are spread rather than grouped: 291 tiles between neighbours at the
median, spanning eight of the map's sixteen quarters.

The doors are the only geometry that changes while a match runs. Twenty-eight
runs, half of them longer than nine tiles and the longest 255, and **every
one of them is attached to a wall**. Not one stands on its own in open
ground, which is worth stating because assuming the opposite is a mistake
that costs a third of the engagements: a fence across a lane is something to
fly around on the way to a fight, where a door on a structure is a way into
somewhere. Six channels means the map does not breathe in and out all
together.

One wormhole. It is a landmark and a hazard; a field of them would be a
physics demonstration.

## Sealed pockets

Six open regions, and one of them is 98.4% of the open ground. The rest are
two pockets of a few thousand tiles and three single tiles, all walled off.

So the original had them too, and it is worth knowing that our own generator
holds itself to a stricter rule than the thing it was measured against:
mapgen joins every stranded piece of the field to the rest and checks for
exactly one region. A pocket is a place a ship can be put and cannot leave,
and 1.6% of a map being that is not a target to reproduce.

The count above is taken one tile at a time, which is the usual way to ask
and is not the question a pilot asks. A hull is three tiles across, so a
structure whose only way in is a one-tile notch counts as open here and is
sealed from the cockpit. Our generator measures its own regions against a
hull instead, and widens or fills whatever that turns up.

## The spawns are not the original's

Worth saying plainly, because it is the one measurement here that turned out
not to be a measurement of the original at all. A `.lvl` file carries no spawn
points, since the original kept them in `arena.conf`. Every spawn tile counted
above was derived by `sim/tools/lvl2vw` from open ground and split north and
south into two home ends. Eight a side is our converter's arithmetic.

That is a good arrangement and mapgen keeps it: a team that spawns among its
opponents never forms up, and eight points spread across the width stop a
roster stacking on one.

## What is not here

Tileset, and therefore how any of it looked. A tile in this core is its
behaviour, so the 160 wall pictures the original distinguished became one
class on the way in. Nothing about the appearance of that map survived the
conversion, and nothing about it is recorded here.
