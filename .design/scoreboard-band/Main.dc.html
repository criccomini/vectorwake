The scoreboard band · round four · the pick
The scoreline, at one size
Chris picked A and asked for one thing changed: nothing on the row varies in size. The first draft set the scores at 22, the clock at 15 and the names at 10; here every figure and every word on the row is 13 points, which is the HUD's own body size and what POS and the feed are already set in. What tells a score from a name from the clock is color and order rather than weight: a side's two words wear its color, the clock is the reading ink, the rating is ink with its movement colored. The row is one key tall as before and the band still grows outward from the clock and stops short of the dial; a phone drops the names and the rating's caption, and the figures always draw. Flags hang under the clock as the radar's beacon; a duel is one kill, so its row is the two pilots either side of the clock and no score; a room that runs forever has no middle at all; and at the whistle both sides stay at their own strength, with the line under the clock, the flags' line during the match, saying what it is counting to at the same size. That caption is a monitor's: on a phone the dial leaves the line under the clock no room for it.
